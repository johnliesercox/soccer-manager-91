## World, projection and conventions

* World space is the scene's, in metres: `X` down the 105 m length, `Y` across the 68 m width (growing towards the near touchline), `Z` up. Scene coordinates convert with `toMetres(x, y)`; the ball's normalised `z` is about three metres at 1 (`BALL_Z_M`).
* The view is 2:1 dimetric: `u = X - Y`, `v = (X + Y)/2`, `sx = ox + P*u`, `sy = oy + P*(v - Z)` (`HEIGHT = 1`, the classic ratio). `P` is pixels per metre. The pitch is therefore a diamond: the X = 0 goal line is the upper-left edge, the X = 105 goal line the lower-right edge, the far touchline (Y = 0) the upper-right edge and the near touchline (Y = 68) the lower-left edge. Home attacks the lower-right goal in the first half.
* Depth for painter's order is `X + Y`; bigger is nearer. Ties break on a stable key.
* `unproject(sx, sy, cam)` returns the ground point under a pixel (`Z = 0`); sprite boxes are used for taps, nearest first.
* Headings: a world facing angle becomes one of `E, SE, S, SW, W, NW, N, NE` by projecting a short forward vector to the screen; `S` faces the viewer. `dir8` takes a hysteresis so a figure does not flicker on a sector edge.
* Scale: three camera modes cycle on the Camera button. `follow`: `P = 16` on stages 700 px wide or more (a two-metre figure is 32 px), 12 on phones. `close`: 24 on a desktop (48 px figures), 16 on a phone. `whole`: fits the ground. The art sheets exist at all three scales; `create({ followP })` still overrides.
* Camera composition: during attacks and goals the centre leans up to a third of the way towards the goal mouth (capped at 12 m), both goal posts are kept inside a 10%/16% margin and the interest point inside an 18%/22% margin; while a shot is in flight the pan cap rises from 1.25 to 3.1 view widths per second (time constant 0.2 s instead of 0.55 s) so that a narrow phone view reaches the goal before the ball does; in open play the centre leads 4 m in the direction of attack. The view may look 12% past the far and left edges (stands), 34% past the near side and 22% past the right end.

## Sprites

`iso-sprites.js` builds a figure from a skeleton in its own metres (forward, sideways, up) with stylised 16-bit proportions (a big head, two metres tall). A pose function moves the joints: `idle`, `walk` (4 frames), `run` (6), `kick` and `shoot` (4: wind-up, contact, follow-through, recover), `receive` (2), `slide` (2), `block`, `dive` (3: launch, full stretch, down), `set`, `celebrate` (2), `booked`, and for the referee `whistle`, `card` and `point`. The dressed figure (legs with boots and sock tops, shorts, torso, collar, arms, hair in three styles, face, eyes, keeper gloves, the referee's card) is turned to the heading's world angle, projected with the same dimetric rule as the pitch, sorted by depth, rasterised into whole pixels with two-tone shading (light from the upper left of the screen) and given a one-pixel outline. Facing away shows the back of the head; facing the viewer shows the face. Kits: shirt from the primary colour with an optional pattern (`stripes`, `hoops`, `quarters`, set in `capture.js` for the clubs that wore them, plain when a clash kit is worn), shorts from the secondary (or a contrasting default when the two are too close), socks matching the shirt, keepers in the swapped colours with gloves, the referee in black. Skin and hair come from `Matchday.sprites.variantFor(id)`, so a player looks the same in every view. Poses that stretch along the ground (`slide`, `dive`) use a wider cell. The cache is capped at 2,400 figures and cleared on dispose.

`iso-actors.js` chooses the pose from the scene: locomotion frames advance with metres covered (never with the wall clock, so a paused or rewound match repeats the same frame), action frames come from `actionPhase`, and a kick, trap, block, slide or dive faces the scene's `aim` so the keeper flies towards the shot. A controlled ball is drawn a touch ahead of its owner's front foot. Anyone standing in the goalmouth in front of the X = 105 goal is lifted above that whole goal in draw order (its net is nearer the viewer than its line, and a keeper seen through the mesh reads as "inside the net"); anyone inside either goal is drawn between the back net and the crossbar, roof strands and near post.

## Renderer frame

1. `iso-camera.update` picks `P` and where the world origin lands (snapping only on cuts, seeks, first frame, stage or mode changes; otherwise a dead zone, exponential approach and a pan cap).
2. The ground (concourse, terraces, crowd, roof, boards, pitch, markings) is painted into 256 px tiles on demand, keyed to a grid fixed on the world origin, and kept in a least-recently-used cache of 48 tiles (12 MB) on a desktop scale or 36 (9 MB) on a phone scale; a tile costs 3 to 4 ms to paint, and only tiles that hold spectators get a second, crowd-standing variant for the goal bounce. One tile set per scale, at most two (follow and whole).
3. Ground shadows for every actor and the ball, and a restrained ring under the player in possession.
4. The depth-sorted items: sprites, the ball (seven cells, nine from `P` 14, with a faint trail to its shadow when airborne), and the two goals split into a back part (far post, crossbar, back and far side net) and a front part (near post and near side net).
5. Tags, the goal overlay (placed in the part of the view away from that goal, a beat after the ball crosses), the cut fade and the fast-forward banner, exactly as in the pixel view.

## Milestone 3A: chances and highlight selection

The brief: a match should watch like a highlights programme, six interesting incidents rather than fifteen passing sequences, a quiet 0-0 shorter and emptier than a 4-3, and nothing that contradicts the official record. 3A is the football logic; the art for the new actions is 3B.

**The chance generator (`timeline.js`).** The timeline still walks the match as possession spells between the official anchors (goals, cards, whistles), but a spell now ends in one of three ways: a *chance* in the final third, a midfield turnover (an interception, a tackle, a clearance hoofed away), or the next anchor. How often a spell goes for a chance is paced by a shot budget drawn once from the official record and the presentation seed: `round(clamp(5 + 2.2·goals + 0.6·cards + U(−2, 3), 4, 18))`, ×1.25 with extra time. The spells then spend it: the chance probability per spell is `(budget − shots so far) / spells left`, so a 0-0 ends up with six or seven shots and a 4-3 with eighteen, and a match never goes far past its budget (crosses are headed clear more often once it is spent). A chance is an *approach* (a through ball, a cross from the flank, a cut-back, a long shot, a corner), a *finish* (kick, first-time, volley, header: the technique decides the ball height it is met at, `TIMELINE_GEOMETRY.HEAD_Z` 1.75 m or `VOLLEY_Z` 0.55 m) and an *outcome* drawn from a table per technique: caught, parried (the ball comes back out: a defender hacks it clear, an attacker follows up first time, or it runs behind for a corner), wide, over, post, bar (both at the real geometry, `POST_Y` 3.66 m from the centre, `BAR_Z` 2.44 m, and the ball visibly comes back off them), blocked (a defender standing in it or sliding), cleared off the line. Corners come off parries, blocks, line clearances and headed clearances and are taken by the next spell, always as a cross into the box (headed clear, claimed, or met by an attacker). Every event of one chance shares a `chanceId`; new event types are `CROSS` (a high ball with `to.z`), `SHOT_POST` (with `woodwork`) and `INTERCEPTION`; shots carry `technique` and their outcome fields (`save`, `miss`, `woodwork`, `slide`, `line`), all optional and validated by the schema.

**Official goals.** A goal is still an official event at its official second with its official scorer; what changed is how the ball gets there. The spell before a goal picks the *kind* of finish first (a through ball struck first time or after a touch 34%, a cross headed in 12%, a cross volleyed 6%, a corner headed in after a teammate's shot was parried behind 6%, a rebound off the keeper or the post 12%, a solo run 10%, a long shot 8%, each only when there is time for it) and paces its build-up passes to leave exactly the room that kind needs, so the last pass reaches the scorer, or the crosser, a few seconds before the finish and nobody dribbles for fifteen seconds. The scorer always applies the final touch; headers come from a cross arriving at head height; a scorer handed the ball early lays it off and gets it back.

**Choreography (`scene.js`, `interactions.js`, `trajectories.js`, `behaviour.js`).** Every event with a `from` and `to` becomes a launch and an arrive keyframe through one `flight()` helper, carrying the launch height, the technique and the arc (a cross 1.3, a pass 1, a shot 0.35, a parry 0.5) and arrival flags (`aerial`, `catch`, `save`, `post`, `block`, `line`, `out`, `loose`, `intercept`). The ball's height in flight is the arc over the straight line from launch height to arrival height, and a ball met in the air keeps its height into an immediate strike. Action cues: `header` and `volley` (±0.45 s around the contact, with the ball height in `actionDetail.z`), `shoot`/`pass`, `catch`, `dive` (also for a beaten keeper, flagged `beaten`), `getup`, `block` (with `slide` and `line` flags), `tackle` (with `slide`), `receive` (suppressed when the ball is struck first time, and clipped before the next kick), `dismay` for the shooter after a miss, the woodwork or a line clearance (`actionDetail.kind`). The planner holds whoever meets a corner at its landing point, opens a corner's set-piece window only once the ball is dead (the last touch), re-aims a cross or pass towards its receiver when he cannot reach it and lets a first-time strike move with its re-aimed arrival, re-aims clearances up to 22 m towards their collector (a clearance now names who picks it up), and brakes everyone after the final whistle. Until the 3B art lands, the renderers borrow poses: a header is the `receive` frame lifted in a jump timed to the contact (the apex is the ball), a volley uses `shoot`, a catch `set` then `block`, `getup` `set` then idle, `dismay` `booked`, a sliding block `slide`.

**Highlight selection (`playback.js`).** `chancesOf(p)` groups a timeline's events by `chanceId` and scores each chance by its most dramatic moment (`DRAMA`: post and bar 10, a line clearance 9, a parry 8, a header 7, a volley 6, a catch 5, a sliding block 5, an interception or a last-ditch tackle 3, wide or over 3, a standing block 2), plus a little for a second shot, a corner or a cross. `buildTimeMap` keeps the must windows (kick-offs, whistles, every goal with its 24 s build-up and 11 s after, every card), then picks chances greedily: the score is discounted for a kind already shown (×0.6 per repeat) and for sitting close to another highlight (×0.5 within two minutes), at most `5 − 0.7·goals` of them (never fewer than two), none below 1.5. A chosen chance is shown whole, from six seconds before its first pass to three and a half after its last event. The real length of the match is `30 + 7 × (goals + chances + cards/2)` seconds, between 50 and 112 (plus 24 for extra time); highlights play at 1.7× to 2.8×, faster only when a goal glut would otherwise run past 125 s (3.5× at most), and the rest fast-forwards. A 0-0 therefore runs about 70 s with five incidents, a 2-1 about 85, a 4-3 about 110. The selection is a pure function of the timeline, so it is deterministic and the same on every device.

**Tests.** `test-highlights.js` (new): shots scale with the record and never run absurd; every shot has a technique and a tagged chance; headers and volleys are met at their heights; the woodwork is struck at the real geometry and the ball comes back; catches are held and parries rebound; line clearances start on the line and blocks happen on the ball's path; goals are finished in every technique, the last pass before a goal reaches the scorer, a headed goal comes from a cross at head height; in the scene, headers, volleys, first-time strikes, catches, woodwork (and the shooter's reaction) and blocks are choreographed as such; the picker is deterministic, keeps every goal, card and whistle, shows each chosen chance whole, prefers drama, varies and spreads its picks, and gives a goalless match a shorter, emptier programme than a goal-laden one. `test-phase25.js` and `test-isometric.js` were extended for the new actions (and the flicker check now counts only pairs close enough to overlap).

## Milestone 3B: the animation set, reactions, crowd, commentary, rules

**Poses (`iso-sprites.js`, `tools/iso-art.js`, Floyo batch 4).** Twelve poses were added to the skeleton after the Milestone 2A fifteen, so every earlier sheet row keeps its place: `header` (crouch, the leap with the head thrown at the ball, landing; the jump itself is added by the renderer, timed to the contact), `volley` (leg back and high, the horizontal contact, the follow-through), `poke` (a standing tackle), `catch` (arms up to meet the ball, clutched to the chest), `parry` (a dive with one hand thrown at the ball, then down), `getup` (on hands and knees, rising on one knee), `kneeslide`, `runcheer` (four running frames with the arms in a V), `hug` (arms out, the hop in), `dismay` (hands on the head), `kneel`, `appeal`. 116 outfield rows (two hair styles) and 58 keeper rows at three scales; the painted cells come from Floyo batch 4 through the same converter (`tools/floyo/README.md`), with the generated figure under any cell that is not painted, which is now a deliberate choice for the few headings a painter cannot read (a body lying along the line of sight).

**Renderers (`iso-actors.js`).** Actions map to the new poses by phase; `actionDetail` carries what a renderer needs beyond the name: the ball height a header meets (the jump is `0.18 m + (ball height − 1.7 m)` up to 0.73 m, apex at the contact), `slide` for a tackle or block, `parry` for a dive, `variant` for a reaction or a celebration. Celebration variants: 0 arms aloft, 1 running with the arms up, 2 the knee slide (it faces the crowd), 3 the hug. Reaction variants: 0 head in hands, 1 down on the knees, 2 appealing.

**Choreography (`scene.js`, `interactions.js`).** A goal's celebration window picks one of four styles from the seed (arms aloft where he stands; a run to the supporters with the others chasing; a short run and the knee slide; a huddle with his teammates), with three teammates arriving, four or five when the goal matters (an equaliser or a lead taken, late on), the camera moving ahead to where the scorer is heading so he runs into frame, the beaten keeper showing it (head in hands or on his knees) and the nearest defender too. Windows carry timed animation segments per player (`anim[id] = [{ from, to, action, extra, aim }]`). A miss, the woodwork or a line clearance draws one of three reactions from the shooter, chosen from the event and the player.

**Crowd (`iso-stadium.js`, `isometric.js`).** The scene exposes `crowd: { teamId, age, intensity }` through a celebration (and the goal flash). The renderer draws the scoring side's painted stands again over the cached ground, each tile in ten horizontal bands lifted by a travelling wave (`drawCrowdReaction`): the home support in the main stand and the X=0 end, the away support in the X=105 end, a wave that builds in a third of a second, dies away over seven, is bigger for a goal that matters, and is skipped under reduced motion. About eighty small `drawImage` calls per frame for a few seconds; nothing is repainted.

**Commentary (`playback.js`, `matchday-ui.js`).** Events carry `say` (spoken as the action starts, at the launch of a flight) as well as `text` (as it resolves): "Kendrick shoots!" at the kick, "Davies holds it." as it lands; passes and crosses are spoken as they are played; a foul has its own line. `linesOf(p, map)` builds the timed lines, adds a lead-in naming the side coming forward at the start of every highlight that does not open on a restart or a whistle, and a build-up line naming the man on the ball when nothing has been said in the six seconds before a shot. The playback fires `onLine` (official lines always, the rest inside highlights) and re-emits the latest line after a seek; the board's commentary now listens to `onLine`, the log and the sounds to `onEvent` as before. No window opens in the middle of a chance: it backs up to the chance's start.

**Bookings.** The scene exposes `cardAt(t)` (from 4.5 s before a card to 2.7 s after); in that window the planner walks the referee to a spot a metre and a half from the offender and holds the offender where he stands, so the card is shown beside the man it is for.

**Misses.** A shot wide goes half a metre to six wide of the post (a header or a volley, up to three and a half), either side; before, it could land twenty metres wide.

**Rules.** A shot off the woodwork that goes behind untouched is a goal kick; a corner off the woodwork only after the keeper gets a hand to the rebound (a `DEFLECTION` with `keeperId`, a line of its own); corners off parries, blocks, line clearances and headed clearances keep the defender's touch in the chain; the ball runs behind for a corner from the moment it sets off (`corner(s, cy, fromPt, fromT)`), never before the clearance that sent it there has landed; a clearance names its collector and never the man who cleared it. `test-highlights.js` section 4 audits a season: no teleports, every corner after a defending touch, every goal kick after an attacking touch, no live ball beyond the lines, no catch that rebounds.

**Selection.** A chance that ends with a corner and the chance the corner is swung into are one sequence in `chancesOf`, so a window that shows the corner won shows what came of it (a test checks every corner shown is followed to its outcome). The variety discount counts outcomes and techniques (a parry, a header, a volley), not the way the ball arrived (a cross, a corner), and the spacing discount measures from goals, cards and chances already picked rather than from the whistles, so a chance in the third minute is not marked down for being near the kick-off.

**Variety.** Each match draws a temperament from the seed (`temper`: keepers, woodwork, misses, blocks, each 0.6 to 1.5) that scales the outcome tables, and the shot budget's noise is wider, so two matches with the same score read differently; everything stays a pure function of the official record and the seed.

## Milestone 3C: the ball meets the man

**The contact audit (`test-contact.js`).** Twenty matches, every contact: launches (pass, cross, clearance, shot, first-time, header, volley), arrivals (first touch, catch, parry, sliding and standing block, goal-line clearance, slide and standing tackle) and the keeper's touch on a rebound. For each, the scene is sampled at the contact second and the ball measured against the contact point of the sprite the renderer draws: the same `poseFor`, `dir8` and skeleton (`iso-sprites.pose`) turned into world metres by the heading, so the audit sees what the viewer sees. The contact point is the leading foot for kicks, slides and pokes, the head for a header, the hands' midpoint for a catch, the reaching hand for a parry, the nearest point of the trunk (shins to chest) for a block with the body. Measured: horizontal distance, height difference, the action and frame on screen, the facing error, and whether the ball is clearly closer at another moment on the wrong side (before an arrival, after a launch). Thresholds are what a clean contact looks like at gameplay scale (a boot's length for a kick, a head's width for a header, exact height for a block). A `REPORT=path` writes every case as JSON; `STRICT=1` asserts the per-kind pass rates.

**Positions are recorded before the step (`trajectories.js`).** The planning loop stored each tick's positions after applying the step planned at that tick, so everything drawn ran a quarter of a second ahead of the plan: receivers arrived early and left as the ball landed, kickers moved off at the kick. `X[k]` now holds the positions the plan at tick `k` was made from.

**The ball ahead of the feet lives in the scene (`scene.displayedBall`).** A controlled ball is `AHEAD` (0.3 m) ahead of its owner's feet along the facing he is drawn with (`facingOf`: the movement facing, or towards the aim of his action), and the blends onto a launch key, a tackle or a block key land exactly on the key point. The isometric renderer no longer adds its own offset.

**Stand-offs (`interactions.STAND`).** Added: `tackle` 0.7 (a slide comes in from the side of the ball's path, from the inside; the cue faces him at the ball along that line, `k.tackleFrom`), `poke` 0.62 (head on), `slide` 0.6 for a sliding block, `block` 0.1, and the catch by height (`catchStand`: 0.46 reaching up above 1.45 m, 0.26 at the chest, 0.3 crouching under 0.8 m). A tackle or a block has a preset control time (`presetControl`, half a second, known before the simulation so the planner sees the ball loose) and the ball lies where it was met until then; the man who made the challenge holds for 0.3 s before collecting. The kicker holds his follow-through for 0.45 s after a launch. A carrier whose ball is not going anywhere before he strikes it (a restart, a ball received where he strikes from) stands off at once rather than in the last second.

**Frames (`iso-actors.poseFor`).** The kick's wind-up runs to phase 0.55 and the contact frame from there (the ball leaves at 0.59). The header's jump meets the ball: `lift = clamp(ballZ - 1.82, 0.1, 0.7)`, apex at the contact; the timeline meets headers between 2.0 and 2.45 m (`headZ()`, one draw per header, shared by the cross and the header). The catch shows `set` then the clutch for a low ball, the clutch throughout at chest height, the reach then the clutch above 1.45 m.

**Bookings (`timeline.js`, `scene.js`, `behaviour.js`, `interactions.js`).** The timeline stops play early enough before a card for the ball to reach the offender's part of the pitch (a reserve of 5 s plus a fifth of the distance, 6 to 16 s), passes it to the man he will foul, who runs at him; the FOUL event is where the ball is. The scene lists `fouls` (offender, victim, spot) and `cardAt` starts after the foul; the planner sprints the offender at the ball to 0.75 m, holds the victim, sends the referee to the offender from the whistle (`pos.ref` is passed to the planner for that; he also sprints when more than 20 m behind play); the cues give the offender a slide and the victim two seconds on his knees. The renderer draws the card as a plain rectangle above the referee's hand; the UI shows a badge over the stage (`#md-badge`) for cards and goals.

**Dives and the airborne ball.** The dive cue (half a second before the ball to 0.8 s after) shows the keeper set until a quarter of a second before the ball, springing, at full stretch as it arrives and down a third of a second later; a shot under 0.9 m is parried low. A ball more than half a metre off the ground draws in front of a man within a stride of it rather than behind his sprite.

**Contact emphasis.** The scene exposes `contact` (the last of: header or volley struck, catch, parry, block, line clearance, woodwork; with its spot, height and age), and the isometric renderer draws a two-frame burst and a fading ring for 0.3 s, skipped under reduced motion.

**Also.** A spell that begins at a kick-off takes the kick-off first (a short pass) before it can turn over; a parry with no time for the scramble becomes a catch; the isometric coverage test samples more frames.

## Milestone 3D: the record, the camera, the celebration, the crowd

**The engine's record (`engine.js`, `reconcileRecord`).** Two corrections to the match record the engine writes, both deterministic and both derived from numbers already drawn, so no result, scorer, booking, statistic or balance changes and every save plays on identically (`test-record.js` proves it against `legacy/engine.v150.js` over four seasons): a goal credited to a player at or after the minute of his own dismissal is moved to a minute before it (`1 + (minute mod (red minute minus 1))`; a goal in extra time pushes the dismissal after it instead; a dismissal in minute 1 is pushed after the goal), and the two bookings of a second-yellow dismissal are put in chronological order. Called after the cards are sorted in `playMatch` and after extra-time goals are credited in a European tie. Saved games are not rewritten: the matchday follows whatever record a match carries, and the audit counts contacts by a dismissed man in an old record apart.

**Framing points (`scene.frameAt`).** The scene says what must be in the picture at every instant, in world terms, so the camera and its audit (`test-camera.js`) share one definition. Each point has a weight: `hard` (never out of the inner view: the ball, or the scorer once the camera has left the net), `must` (kept in view and counted when the scale is chosen) or `nice` (kept when it costs nothing). The ball always; the man about to strike and both posts from 2.6 s before a shot until the keeper, the net, the woodwork or the line has dealt with it; the landing point and the goalmouth while a cross is in the air; the man coming in and the man on the ball around a challenge; the offender, the man he fouled and the referee (at 1.8 m, the card) from the foul to the card; through a celebration the scorer, where he is running to (so a run to the supporters or a knee slide has room in front of it) and the two teammates nearest him. The per-frame scene carries `frame`, `frameAt` (for looking ahead), `celebration` ({ cut, end } once the camera is on the scorer) and `lastCut`.

**The camera (`iso-camera.js`).** `update` composes the interest point (led 0.45 s towards where it is going, 0.6 s through a goal), then clamps the centre into the intersection of the intervals the framing points allow (hard first, narrowed by must, then nice), then clamps to the ground, and moves there on a critically damped spring (`OMEGA` 4.2, a cap of 1.1 widths a second, 2.2 times that while a shot flies); the hard points are held after the spring. It snaps at a cut: the start of a highlight (`sinceCut`), a scene cut (`scene.lastCut`: a half placed rather than run to, and the restart after a goal), the cut to the scorer when he is more than 0.22 widths from the goalmouth, a resize, a change of scale. `chooseP` picks the scale once per highlight by sampling `frameAt` over the highlight (the close scale when the must points fit, the wide one when they do not), and again at `celebration.cut` for the celebration alone, so a goal from distance is watched at the wide scale and its celebration at the close one. `state.snapped` and `state.cutKind` tell the renderer what happened; a cut inside a highlight dips through black for a quarter of a second, like the fade into one. `matchday-ui` passes the current highlight (`segment`) to the renderer; `playback` keeps `segA/segB/segFF`.

**Celebrations.** The hold until the restart: the celebration keeps the camera (`celebrationAt`) through the half second between the window closing and the kick-off, and the restart is a cut. The ball is hard for 1.2 s (the net), then the picture cuts or pans to the scorer (`must` for half a second while the camera reaches him, `hard` after).

**The crowd (`iso-stadium.drawCrowdReaction`).** The scoring side's painted stands rise in a wave that runs along the stand (`WAVE`: amplitude 0.3 of a metre at the stand's scale, building in 0.3 s, gone in 8), drawn as sixteen horizontal bands per section, each displaced by its row window (`WAVE_ROWS`: the main stand's lower half, under its roof; the ends and the near terrace edge to edge) so that neither roofs nor front walls move. The home support is the main stand, the X=0 end and the near terrace (whose hoardings are redrawn in front of it); the away support the X=105 end. Camera flashes (`flashbulbs`) scatter white points over the fans for six seconds, hashed from the section and the moment so they are the same everywhere and cost nothing to store, and placed only where the piece has paint (`isoAssets.pieceMask`, a 48 by 48 alpha grid made once per piece). The generated crowd under the painted pieces jumps on the beat for the first five seconds. All of it is skipped under reduced motion.

**The timeline, after the audit.** Three choreography defects the audit found in new fixtures: before a booking, when the offender's own side has the ball, the other side now wins it with a tackle where the ball is (it used to change hands silently, and the man booked kicked a ball twelve metres away); a spell-ending tackle or clearance is given to a man who can reach the ball by then (`lastAt`, where each man was last put by an event, and `defenderNearAt`) rather than to the nearest by formation; a dismissed keeper is replaced in goal by the same defender the scene puts there (`gkOf` reads the men still on the pitch). A slide tackle on a man taking his first touch (the ball not yet moved on) comes in across the line the ball arrived on; the keeper's touch on a woodwork rebound is a parry where the ball reaches him, 1.7 s after the shot. And the long-range goal: a scripted goal by a deep man (a defender, a wide man) used to pass to where he stood, forty or fifty metres out, and he shot from there (a fifth of all goals came from over thirty metres, the longest from seventy-five). A named receiver standing well behind or wide of the point wanted is now played in ahead of himself, up to thirty metres and into the channel (`receiverFor`; the planner starts his run nine seconds before the ball arrives), a scripted build-up's passes go to men not standing behind the ball (`nearestAhead`), and the long-range finish carries in towards goal. Two per cent of goals now come from over thirty metres, the longest from forty-three.

**Tests.** `test-record.js` (the engine corrections against the 1.5.0 engine), `test-camera.js` (the camera over the highlights of a dozen matches at 60 fps on a desktop and a phone stage: the ball in view, the striker and the ball at the strike, both posts at the arrival, the offender and the referee through a booking, the scorer through a celebration, both men at a challenge, the man on the ball, never an empty pitch, the pan cap, the scale changing only at a cut, celebrations at the close scale), `test-contact.js` thresholds unchanged and every kind passing, `test-isometric.js` D02 skips the camera's own cuts.

## Milestone 3D correction pass: restarts, corners and the corners of the ground

**Kick-offs are placed (`scene.js`, `trajectories.placeAll`, `behaviour.js`, `interactions.ballAt`).** Every restart from the centre spot after the first is a cut (`cut: true` on the key), so the kick-off after a goal is placed like a new half rather than run to: `placeAll` puts each side in its own half in its formation squeezed towards its own goal (a forward on the halfway line, `ax = min(0.47, shape.x*0.68)`), the receiving side outside the centre circle (`CIRCLE_M` 9.15 plus a step), the taker on the ball and his partner a stride beside him, the referee a few metres off. The ball is `placed`: `ballAt` holds a restart's ball exactly on its spot from the restart key to the kick, `displayedBall` leaves a placed ball alone, and the planner holds everyone but the taker and his first receiver still until it is taken. The timeline takes every kick-off the same way, a beat after the whistle (`kickOffTap`: 1.0 s on the spot, then a short pass to the partner the scene places beside the taker), before any spell, goal build-up or booking can touch the ball (`kickOffFirst`). The camera's cuts are now simply the scene's (`cutTimes`). The celebration window closes half a second before the kick-off and the camera holds the scorer until the cut.

**Corners from the arc (`timeline.js`: `CORNER_AX`, `CORNER_Y`, `cornerY`).** The ball lies 0.6 m in from each line (0.85 m from the flag, inside the one-metre quarter circle) at the flag on the side of the goal line the ball crossed: a parry behind on the keeper's side, a woodwork rebound turned round the post on that side, a header behind on the header's side, the scripted corner goal on the parry's side (the rebound's random landing point used to choose the flag). The taker is a man who can be at the flag by the kick (`nearestAt`, from the last place an event put him), sprints there (the corner window's band), and stands a boot behind the ball, a stride off the pitch (the planner's clamp allows 0.8 m beyond the lines); the ball waits placed until the cross. A corner comes 3.5 s after the ball is dead, 5.5 s after a header behind.

**No silent moves.** When a spell ran out of time a turnover used to put the ball 25 to 40 m away without an event (the hacked clearance there was no time to show); the ball now stays where it is, with the keeper or the defender who had reached it (`turnoverTo` at the spot). The scene's launch clamp had been hiding most of these; the event record is honest now too.

**The corners of the ground (`iso-stadium.js`: `CORNERS`, `cornerTerrace`, `cornerTower`).** A filled quarter terrace in each corner, in the notch between the end of one stand and the start of the next: twelve rows parallel to the diagonal, each a little longer and higher than the one in front (rising at the pace of the lower neighbour, so the main stand's end shows above it), the same coats-and-heads crowd as the generated terraces, a crush barrier every fourth row, a low wall at the front. At each junction a concrete block: a stair tower in the far corners (9.5 m by the main stand and the X=0 end, 4.8 m by the X=105 end), a turnstile block in the near corners (2.6 m, below the hoardings' line of sight to the pitch). The two floodlight pylons stand behind the far corner terraces; there are none in the near corners, where in this view they would stand between the viewer and the corner flags. The far corners are painted after the painted stand ends and before the pitch; the near corners after the hoardings. All of it is in the ground tiles (painted once per tile), so it costs nothing per frame and can never cover a player or the ball; nothing stands inside the run-off, and `test-isometric.js` E01 checks the footprints and that no tower rises into the projected pitch.

**Tests.** `test-restarts.js` (new): over two league weeks, every kick-off and corner in the scene: the conceding side restarts; the celebration closed and a cut at every restart after a goal; everyone but the taker in his own half; the receiving side outside the circle; the ball on the spot and still until the kick; nobody drifting before it; a beat before every kick; corners at all four flags, on the side the ball went out, inside the arc and still until the kick, the taker at the ball. `test-matchday.js` and `test-playback.js` treat a placed restart as a cut in their teleport checks and allow a stride beyond the lines; `test-isometric.js` E01 as above.

## Option E correction pass: dead balls and locomotion

**A ball that goes behind is dead until the restart (`timeline.js`: `goalKick`, `hardStop`, `restartHeld`, `winBack`; `interactions.ballAt`; `behaviour.js`).** A shot that went wide or over used to earn a goal kick only when six seconds remained before the spell's deadline; otherwise possession was quietly turned over to a defender at the edge of his box and the scene rolled the dead ball from behind the goal to his feet (and, even with a goal kick, the ball rolled "loose" from the goal line to the six-yard line). Now every wide or over shot, and every deflection that runs behind off the woodwork, is followed by a goal kick about three seconds after the ball went out: the keeper places it, then rolls it out to a defender when there is time, and otherwise holds the placed ball, so a scripted goal build-up or a card that follows starts from the restart (the other side cuts out the goal kick in midfield, `winBack`, instead of tackling a keeper holding a dead ball). When the whistle comes first the half ends with the ball dead. In the scene an out ball runs on for a moment with what is left of its pace (two to three metres, at least a metre beyond the goal line) and lies there, `dead`, uncarried and unpressed, until the restart key places it; a ball the timeline has run to the corner flag lies at the flag. A corner awarded just before a stop is taken short (tagged to its chance so the window shows it) before anyone can tackle at the flag, and a restart nobody takes stays placed. The shot budget now counts the saved or woodwork shot inside a scripted goal, with hard caps once it is spent, so a match's shot count is bounded rather than lucky.

**Locomotion (`iso-actors.js`: `STANDING`, `poseFor`; `iso-camera.js`: `state.sceneCut`; `trajectories.js`: `SET_OFF`, `PULL_UP`, `PRECISE`; `scene.js`).** Three things made players glide. An action drawn standing (receive, set, celebrate, dismay, booked, block, getup) overrode locomotion for its whole cue, so a receiver ran two metres in the trap pose and the celebrating side converged with their arms up: while a man is still travelling at 0.7 m/s or more he is drawn walking or running (a celebrant runs with his arms up), and the action pose takes over once he has slowed. The stride counter dropped any step over three metres as a cut, which froze the legs of a sprinter at the fastest fast-forward; it now keys off the camera's cut signal and accepts steps to twelve metres. And the planner let everyone creep after the drifting team shape at 0.1 to 0.6 m/s, below walking pace, for 23% of all player-frames, which the scene called `idle`: a man who has stopped now sets off only once his target is a metre away and pulls up within 30 cm (the carrier, a receiver meeting the ball, set-piece placement and a dismissed man walking off keep exact steering), so creeping falls to 2% and movement comes in bursts of about six metres with pauses of a second or two. A dismissed man's movement is no longer forced to `idle` while he walks off, and a celebrant told to run cheers on the spot once he has arrived.

**Painted art before the first frame (`iso-assets.js`: `styleReady`, `isReady`, `expects`; `isometric.js`: `ready`, `holdingFrame`, `preload`; `matchday-ui.js`: `startViewer`; `ui.html`: `init`).** The sheets used to decode on the first mount, and for the second or two that took the viewer drew the generated figures in place of the painted ones. The default style is now decoded first and started at boot (an idle callback on the title screen), each style reports when all its sheet sets have landed, the renderer draws a plain holding frame ("Preparing the ground...") rather than anything generated while its style is still decoding, and the match screen waits for `ready()` (six seconds at most) before it starts the clock, so nothing of the match is missed. The headless harness fails image loads at once so the viewer never waits in tests.

**Tests.** `test-deadball.js` (new): over 192 matches, every miss and every deflection behind is followed by a goal kick from the defending side's goal area or a corner only after a defender's touch, or by the whistle, with nothing touching the ball in between; in the scene the dead ball never moves more than its run-on, is never carried, and only comes back inside the pitch on a restart. `test-locomotion.js` (new): the scene stepped the way the isometric renderer steps it (its own distance accumulator and pose selection), at 60 and 30 frames a second, speeds 1, 2 and 4, and the highlight fast-forward rates: no man travels half a second at walking pace or faster without legs, none runs over a metre and a half on a frozen leg frame, none runs on the spot.

## Option E polish pass: the crowd and the goals (Stage B)

**The goals (`tools/visual-rnd/blender_pieces.py`: `build_goal`, `surface`, `net_material`; `iso-stadium.js`: `netState`, `drawGoal`, `placedPiece`; `isometric.js`; `build.py`).** The goal is modelled once in Blender in world units and shipped as placed pieces, like the corners and pylons. Three parts per end: `goal<end>s` is the frame's shadow on the grass alone (a shadow catcher with every object casting but unseen), drawn right after the ground and before any actor, so a man standing in the goalmouth stands on it; `goal<end>b` and `goal<end>f` are the back and front halves split exactly as the old raster goal was, so the depth order of keeper, ball and net is unchanged (the back half at `backDepth`, the front at `frontDepth`, players and ball sorted between them). The nets are surfaces carrying a procedural grid rather than a hedge of tubes: one translucent sheet whose strands stay six tenths of a pixel at every scale, the roof sagging, the back net bellying, the side nets drawn in, over round posts with rounded joints and sockets, angled stanchions, rear uprights and a back rail. The back net has seven states: still, and the net pushed out where a goal went in, in three lanes across the goal (by the far post, the middle, by the near post) and two phases (bulge, settle). `netState` picks one from what the scene already exposes: the goal flash (its age is the clock, since the ball reaches the net on the goal's own second) and the ball lying dead beyond that goal line, by its `y`; bulge to 0.4 s, settle to 0.9 s, still after. A save, a block, the woodwork and a ball over the bar never move the net, because none of them is a goal flash with a dead ball behind the line. `placedPiece` falls back from the state piece to the still piece, and from that to the raster goal, so a style without the pieces (B in production) draws as before. The net pieces are lossy WebP (the build takes `.webp` before `.png` for any piece); the shadow is PNG with its alpha quantised. The goal's shadow falls on the pitch side of the goal line because the scene's light comes from the near touchline, as it does for every figure.

**The crowd (`blender_pieces.py --crowd 2`; `make_runs_pieces.py --prompt-style`; `paint_pieces.py`).** The spectators are a second generation of geometry (shoulders, arms, scarves, hats, children, a few raised arms, seated rows under the roof) painted through Floyo by the crowd-only route, in two treatments put to John as styles E and F of one review build (`ISO_STYLES=B,E,F ISO_DEFAULT=E`; `matchday-ui.js` places `isometric-f` one tap after the plain Iso view). The stands remain still pictures: the crowd is club-neutral and the painted terraces do not rise, only the generated crowd under them jumps in any gap.

**Tests.** `test-matchday-ui.js` knows the comparison view's place in the cycle. The seventeen suites were run after the goal change (all pass); the goal pieces add no test of their own because the renderer path is the existing placed-piece path with a name chosen per frame, exercised by `test-isometric.js` and `test-playback.js`.

## Milestone 4: touch selection and the picture when nothing is playing

**Selection.** `pick(items, X, Y, slop)` in `renderers/isometric.js` is pure and exported on the renderer definition (with `TOUCH_SLOP` 14 and `POINTER_SLOP` 4, in canvas pixels). It works on the items of the last frame, so it is right at any scale and after any pan with no inverse projection of its own: every player whose drawn box is within `slop` of the tap is a candidate, and the one whose body is nearest wins (the body is taken at 60% down its box, above the feet anchor); a dead heat goes to the figure drawn later. The tap handler chooses the slop from the event's `pointerType`, falling back to the device's `(pointer: coarse)` when an old browser sends none. Nothing about selection is stored anywhere but the renderer: the scene, the presentation and the save are untouched.

**Still frames.** Frames normally come from the playback loop through `render(scene, hud)`. Four things can need a frame when the loop is not running (paused, or at full time): a tap, a change of size, a camera change, and a cut that was drawn mid-fade and never followed. The renderer tells the two cases apart by the time since the last loop frame (the loop's frames carry `hud.sinceCut`; 300 ms without one means nothing is playing) and then draws a *still frame*: the stored scene with `still: true`, which skips the dip and the fade, and `dtReal: 0`, which `iso-camera.update` now treats as no time passing (the spring is not stepped). While the loop is running none of this happens: a tap waits for the loop's next frame, and a fade is left to finish on its own. A frame returned by `drawFrame` reports `fading`, and `render` arms a 350 ms timer on a fading frame that draws the still frame if no other frame has come; `dispose` cancels it.

A 3D renderer needs the same three rules and nothing from this code: redraw on resize, complete a transition that no frame follows, and pass no time to the camera on a redraw that is not a frame.

**The match screen.** `matchday-ui.js` redraws the paused picture 120 ms after a window resize for whichever view is mounted (Pixels, Markers and Board clear their canvas on a resize and do not redraw themselves; their code is unchanged), labels Camera and View through two small functions (short wording below 480 px, the full wording as `aria-label`) and does not raise badges for the events Skip jumps past. The 44 px buttons and the 14 px the stage gives up are two media queries for a screen held upright; a phone in landscape is deliberately outside both.

## Milestone 5: what changed in the pipeline, and why a 3D renderer cares

**Ends.** `MD.homeDirAt(p, second)` in `schema.js` is the one rule for which way the home side attacks: ends change at half-time, for extra time and at its interval. `timeline.js` (`attackDir`) and `scene.js` (`dirAt`) both ask it, so events and positions cannot disagree. A renderer reads `scene.attackDir` as before.

**Kits.** `capture.js` settles the two sides' kits for the match: a striped, hooped or quartered shirt counts as two colours (`MD.shirtColours`), the away side changes if any colour of one shirt is close to any colour of the other (`MD.kitsClash`, the long-standing 110 measure), and a shirt that is all pitch-green is changed whichever side wears it. This is in the presentation, so every view shows it. The goalkeepers and the referee are dressed by the isometric renderer alone, in `iso-actors.js` (`kitsFor(teams)`, remembered per match): the referee wears the first of a list (black first) that stands 110 clear of every shirt colour on the pitch, and each keeper the first of a list that stands 110 clear of the opposing shirt, 90 of his own side's and of the referee, 100 of the grass, and 70 of the other keeper. Colours come as `#rrggbb` or `hsl()` (`MD.rgbOf` reads both). A 3D renderer should call `kitsFor` (or copy its rule) rather than dress keepers in the club's second colour, which is what clashed.

**When a view fails.** `mountRenderer` in `matchday-ui.js` tries the view asked for and then Pixels, Markers and Board in turn; a view that throws while a match is being shown is marked broken for that match and the next takes over at the same second (`viewFailed`); only if nothing can draw does the viewer close onto the result with a message. The stored choice of view is never changed by a fallback. If the painted art has not arrived six seconds after the match screen opens, the renderer is told to `proceed()` and draws with the generated figures and stands wherever a painted one is missing. A new renderer gets all of this for nothing so long as it throws rather than drawing rubbish.

**Working ahead of a cut.** Two costs used to land on the frame of a cut: the camera choosing its scale for the highlight (`chooseP` samples the scene up to eighty times) and a screenful of ground tiles being painted. Both are now done beforehand, a little a frame, on frames from the playback loop only:

* `iso-camera.js` remembers the scale per match and highlight (a `WeakMap` on the scene's `frameAt`), and `planJob(...)` works the same answer out for about three milliseconds a frame.
* The match screen names the next cut to the view with `renderer.prepare(sceneAtTheCut, { segment })`: a cut inside the highlight (a restart from `scene.cutTimes`, or the cut to a scorer from `scene.celebration.cut`) from three and a half match seconds before it; otherwise the start of the next highlight, from the last six match seconds of the current one and through the fast-forward. It also calls `renderer.expectWave()` four match seconds before a goal.
* The renderer finishes the scale, works out where the camera will stand on a scratch camera (the live one is not touched), marks those tiles to be kept, and paints the missing ones one a frame, with their risen-crowd variants when a goal is on its way. With nothing queued it paints the tiles just off screen, the edge the camera is heading for first.
* The tile cache is sized to the stage (the view, the next view and the ring round the screen: 30 to 40 tiles on a phone, up to 72 on the largest desktop stage, per scale) and gives up its least recently drawn tile that is not being kept.

None of this changes a pixel (`test-ahead.js` compares frames with and without it). `prepare` and `expectWave` are optional methods: the other three views do not have them and the match screen checks before calling.

**The measuring screen.** `What's new > Measure the match frame rate` sets `Matchday.ui.measuring`; the next match watched times every animation frame for sixty seconds of play and every call to `renderer.render`, then pauses and shows the figures (`Matchday.ui.lastMeasure`). Nothing is stored or sent.

## The phone smoothness pass (17 November 2026): where a frame's time goes on a phone

John's Galaxy S23 Ultra averaged 49.9 frames a second in the 1.7.0 preview, with one frame in thirty at 50 ms or more. Headless Chromium at the phone's size with the processor slowed six times reproduces those figures (`tools/phone-probe.js`), and its frame log named three causes. Each is dealt with at its root, and none changes a pixel: `tools/tile-reference.js`, `tools/sprite-reference.js` and `tools/frame-reference.js` compare every ground tile, 61,248 player pictures and 198 whole frames with the build before the pass, and `test-smooth.js` holds the same in Node.

**A correction to the Milestone 5 section above.** It says the match screen calls `renderer.expectWave()` before a goal and prepares the cut to the scorer. Neither happened in the game. `expectWave` was called only by `test-ahead.js`, which carried its own copy of the match screen's rule, and the scorer's cut was looked for in the scene at 0.05 s after the goal, 1.15 s before the scene knows of one. So the frame of a goal painted the risen stands at once, and the cut to the scorer painted a screen of ground at the other scale: the third-of-a-second freeze on the phone. The rule now lives in one function that both the game and the tests call:

* `MD.lookAhead(scene, goalTimes, segsOf)` in `playback.js` returns `look(at, state)`, which answers `{ wave, target, seg }`: a goal within four match seconds, the match second the next cut lands on (a cut inside the highlight, the scorer's cut, or the start of the next highlight), and that cut's highlight. `matchday-ui.js` asks it on every frame and calls `renderer.expectWave()` and `renderer.prepare(sceneAtTheCut, { segment, sceneAt })`; `test-ahead.js` and `test-smooth.js` ask the same function, and `test-ahead.js` now checks that the frame the ball goes in on, and the frame of the scorer's cut, paint no more than one tile.

**A tile costs what is in it.** `drawGround` paints the whole stadium, and it was called once a tile with the canvas left to discard what fell outside: about 45 ms a tile at phone speed, 141 tiles a minute. Now:

* `ISO.pitch.within(clip, fn)` names the tile's rectangle to the primitives for the length of one layer. `poly` leaves out a polygon whose box misses it, `Raster.line` a line whose box misses it and then each pixel that does, `drawGrass` the grass when the pitch misses it. In `terrace` the eight crowd strips of a stand are looked up once and not once a place, and a row or a place is passed over when the largest strip could not reach the tile. A pixel of margin is kept everywhere. The clip must be the whole of the canvas being painted. `ISO.pitch.culling(false)` turns all of it off, which is how `test-smooth.js` paints each tile both ways.
* `ISO.stadium.groundSteps(...)` is `drawGround` as a list of its eleven layers. A tile painted on demand runs them all at once (`world.get`). A tile painted ahead runs them inside what is left of the frame: `world.start(tx, ty, jump)` and `world.advance(now, until)` in `buildWorld`, driven by `paintAhead(frameStart)` in the renderer, which stops when the frame has used 9 ms since its drawing began and is not begun on a frame whose own drawing has already used that (a device too slow ever to have time left gets one layer every sixth frame). The tile joins the cache when its last layer is on; if it is wanted on screen before then, `get` finishes it there and then.
* With a goal on its way, the risen painting of the stands in view comes first in the work ahead, and then that of the tiles just off screen, because the camera is still closing on the goal as the ball goes in.

**A player's picture costs a table look-up.** A picture was made by drawing its cell of the sheet onto a small canvas and reading the pixels back, and again for the pattern of a striped shirt; a phone makes about 3,500 in the first minute (it holds two scales), each a read-back from a five-megapixel image that the browser may have to decode again first. Now:

* `MD.isoAssets.prime(scale)` reads the player, pattern and keeper sheets of one scale once, in bands of 96 rows with a pause between them, into one byte a pixel: 0 for a clear pixel, 1 to 112 for material and tone, 113 and up for a shirt pixel with the pattern sheet's value. `get` writes a picture from those bytes and the kit's table (worked out once a kit and look, `tableFor`) with nothing drawn and nothing read back. A sheet with a half-clear pixel or a material outside the table keeps the old way, as does a picture asked for before its sheet is read (`planeState(scale)` says which: `ready`, `old way`, `reading`, `not read`).
* `MD.renderers.isometric.preload` primes scale 16 and then 12 or 24 by the width of the window while the player is in the menus; the renderer primes its stage's two scales when it is mounted or resized, and `ready()` waits for them. The cost is memory: about 11 MB on a phone and 22 MB on a desktop stage, for as long as the page is open.
* The cache of pictures gives up the one longest unused (it gave up the oldest made).

**Pictures are made before they are shown.** `renderer.warm(sceneAt, from, span, segment)` runs the first three seconds of the match through the actors with an animation state of its own before the clock starts (about 190 pictures), and the work ahead of a cut does the same for the second and a half after it, one moment of the scene a frame, after that cut's ground is painted. Both only ask `ISO.actors.build` for its items and throw them away; the pictures stay in the cache.

**The measuring screen** now counts a frame as slower than 30 a second when it is longer than 34 ms, and calls the target met when the slowest 5% are 33 ms or less to the nearest millisecond: a frame of exactly two refreshes of a 60 Hz screen is 33.3 ms, reads as 33.3 or 33.4, and is 30 a second, not slower. The count of frames longer than 50 ms is unchanged and is the one to compare across the two rules.

A 3D renderer would not have ground tiles or recoloured sprite cells, but the three ideas carry over: the match screen says what is coming (`MD.lookAhead`), work ahead is done inside what is left of a frame, and anything heavy that a moment needs is made before that moment.

## Phase 4 (true 3D) readiness

A WebGL renderer would replace steps 1 to 4 with its own projection and meshes and keep step 5. It would import nothing from the isometric files except, optionally, `iso-projection.toMetres` and `BALL_Z_M`; `scene.js` still carries no screen-space values. The world image cache, the sprite cache and the camera state are all private to this renderer and die with `dispose()`.

## Visual styles (Milestone 2A)

Painted art (the Floyo trial, after Milestone 2A): `iso-assets.register` lays `overrides-<spr>.png` and `overrides-keepers-<spr>.png` over the generated sheets cell by cell (a painted cell replaces the generated cell outright), and `standTexture(P)` returns a painted far-stand segment that `iso-stadium.paintedStand` tiles over the generated far stand (scaled from the nearest shipped scale when the camera is at another one). The sheets are decoded once per page (`assetLoad`), and a renderer mounted while that is in flight repaints its tiles when it lands. `ART-PIPELINE.md` has the loop.

The renderer takes `create({ style })`: `'B'` (the 16-bit direction: five tones, rim shading, dark outline, textured grass), `'A'` (the Amiga direction: three tones, black outline, flat bright pitch) or `'none'` (the Milestone 2 procedural art). While the direction is being chosen, the View button also lists `Iso A` and `Iso M2`; the shipped game will keep one. Sheets for both styles are generated from the same model and poses, so switching the whole game's look is a build flag (`ISO_STYLES`).

### Visual R&D styles (the test day before Milestone 4)

Four styles share one renderer in the comparison build (`ISO_STYLES=B,C,D,E`): B the shipped art, C the same references painted by Qwen Image 2512 + ControlNet, D a Blender figure driven by the game's own skeleton (`tools/visual-rnd/joints.json`, exported from `iso-sprites`) and rendered in the game's projection (`tools/visual-rnd/isocam.py`), E the Blender renders painted by the Qwen route. Three mechanisms carry it, all inert in a one-style build:

* **Inheritance.** A style folder may hold an `inherit` file naming its parent; `build.py` takes any file the style lacks from the parent chain and, when the parent ships in the same build, writes an `@parent/spr/field` reference instead of a second copy. `iso-assets.load` resolves the references and decodes each shared image once (`test-isometric` E08).
* **Placed pieces.** A style may ship a texture whose json carries `anchorWorld`; `iso-stadium.placedPiece` draws it by that world anchor at the texture's scale. The goal parts (`goal<end><b|f>`, in place of the vector net, keeping the back and front parts and their depth keys, E09), the four corner terraces and towers (`cornerA`..`cornerD`, over the generated corner, which still runs so the crowd's random stream is unchanged) and the two far floodlights (`pylonA`, `pylonB`) use it. The dugout and the four stands use the piece and stand mechanisms that already existed.
* **A default style for a review build.** `ISO_DEFAULT=E` makes the plain Iso view draw with style E (`Matchday.isoDefaultStyle`) and offers the other inlined R&D styles on the view button without `?dev`; anything E lacks still comes from B through inheritance.
* **Views.** `isometric-c`, `isometric-d`, `isometric-e` are registered like `isometric-a`, offered only under `?dev` and only when the build inlines the style; `?view=<id>` opens in a view once on arrival.

`tools/iso_paint.py` now clears a sheet cell before writing a converted cell into it. The write-up is `PHASE-3-VISUAL-RND.md`; the kit is described in `tools/visual-rnd/README.md`.

### Style E in production (the Option E checkpoint)

After the sign-off, style E became a complete art set rather than a test: every pose and frame of `sprites.js` in eight headings and three scales, in three hair styles (its own `players-<spr>.json` and `patterns-<spr>.png` carry hair 0, 1, 2 where B knows two), the keeper set, the referee's poses, the four stands and four corners as painted textures, and the goal parts and dugout from D copied into its own folder, so `E/inherit` now names B directly and the chain is one step. Nothing in the renderer changed for it: the same manifests, the same `overrides` mechanism, the same feet lines and contact points, so `test-contact` and the animation consistency checks run on E as they ran on B. The scratch style `Xt` is gone. The review build is `ISO_STYLES=B,C,E ISO_DEFAULT=E` (E draws the plain Iso view, C is a view away, B is the fallback inside E); `test-matchday-ui` derives the view cycle from what the build ships, so it passes on both the review and the production build. The checkpoint report is `PHASE-3-OPTION-E-CHECKPOINT.md`.

## Known limitations at Milestone 2A

* Since Milestone 5 a cut paints no ground on its own frame and does not stop to choose its scale (see above). What is left: the very first frame of a match paints its whole screen (before the clock starts), a seek paints the screen it lands on, and on the largest desktop stage a fast pan just after a cut can still paint up to five tiles in a frame.
* The figures are generated from a model and lit, not hand-painted: consistent and animatable, but without hand-drawn character (see `ART-PIPELINE.md`, "Where the ceiling is").
* The preview with both styles at three scales is 3.4 MB; the shipped game would carry one style (about 1.1 MB of sheets).
* The near side of the ground is deliberately shallow (boards, a rail, three rows), so the camera stops short of it and the concourse colour shows beyond; the camera keeps the near-right goal low in the frame for the same reason.
* Headings are eight-way; a dive or slide seen head-on (N or S) foreshortens to a compact shape.
* Phone landscape (844 x 390) gives a 203 px tall stage. Milestone 4 measured it and left it as it is by John's decision: a lower scale does not help (see the limitations in `PHASE-3-QA-REPORT.md`); it needs work on the camera bounds and the layout.
* Frame pacing is measured in headless Chromium, not yet on a real phone.
